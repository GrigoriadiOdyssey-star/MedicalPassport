# MASTER19: Audit MVP implementation specification

Scope is the existing adult single-allergy-disclosure profile of CORE-CAP-AUDIT: internal append and patient receipt query only. This profile may be implementation-ready independently of the entire Audit owner. Investigation/export, legacy import, raw change history, minors, clinical writeback, emergency overrides, AI and prescribing remain outside this profile and stay blocked in the full-owner registry. Readiness here means sufficient engineering design to begin implementation; it does not certify runtime, legal or clinical deployment. MASTER19 explicitly replaces the requirement for already obtained external approvals as a prerequisite to engineering readiness; historical MASTER18 gates remain unchanged.

This delta closes design choices previously deferred in audit-readiness16.md and audit-receipt-query18.md. Purpose, actors, permission matrix, closed append/receipt/error schemas, immutable fields, patient receipt pagination, sensitive-data minimization, lifecycle and failure semantics are inherited from those documents. No source requirement or broader profile is removed.

## Transaction and data ownership decisions

Use a modular application with one relational transaction boundary for this profile's grant row, append-only audit event, deduplication record, receipt projection and outbox. The implementation target is PostgreSQL-compatible transactions with row locking and unique constraints; no microservice split or distributed commit is required. Database product/release/security deployment selection is a separately tracked integration decision; it cannot weaken these semantics. Store tables AuditEvent, AuditReceipt, AuditDeduplication, GrantAuditProjection and AuditOutbox owned by Audit; Consent owns grant state. Composite keys include tenant and grant/patient scope; never join by opaque ID alone. Enforce unique tenant+producer+event_id and tenant+grant+projection_sequence. Append values conform to audit-envelope16.schema.json; patient responses conform to audit-receipt-query18.schema.json. Store source event digest and chain metadata internally; do not expose tenant-wide sequence through the patient API.

All append and audited grant-lifecycle transactions acquire locks in the same order: tenant integrity-stream row, grant row, then producer deduplication key. The tenant stream owns the MASTER16 tenant-scoped ingest sequence and chain predecessor; different grants or producers in one tenant must not create competing chain heads. Acquire the grant row lock before checking current grant/source/policy versions and before allocating its audit projection sequence. Verify current patient and role/policy adapters as described below; authorization is checked again at the controlled response-release boundary. A stale or unavailable decision aborts. Append event, dedupe key, receipt and outbox atomically; no 2xx before commit. Duplicate same-key/same-body returns the original receipt; changed body conflicts. Receipt query captures the projection snapshot before recording its own attempt, avoiding infinite self-audit. Outbox consumers may retry; their idempotency keys and source versions prevent duplicate projections.

Revocation uses the same lock ordering and increments grant version. For clinical disclosure, a response-release admission step must serialize with grant revocation; a read admitted before revocation may complete its already admitted response, while admissions after revocation commit deny. Patient receipt queries instead require current patient binding and audit.receipts.read_self authorization: ACTIVE, REVOKED and EXPIRED clinical grants remain queryable under the MASTER18 accountability contract. Grant revocation must not erase patient access to historical receipts. Patient-account suspension, receipt-policy denial and binding changes invalidate receipt admission independently of clinical grant status. Document the applicable boundary in the UI: revocation cannot recall previously admitted/delivered data. Admission has a fixed maximum 5-second lifetime; expiry aborts and reauthorizes before release. This is an engineering timeout, not a clinical or legal duration. Runtime concurrency tests must prove these properties; they are not proven by this specification. Streaming and asynchronous clinical payload delivery are excluded from the slice.

## Ports and trust boundaries

Identity port returns authenticated subject, tenant, patient binding, adult status, issuer, verification time and binding version. Security port returns ALLOW/DENY/UNAVAILABLE with exact subject/action/resource/purpose, policy version and validity bound. Consent port returns current grant binding/version and lifecycle. No adapter is trusted merely because it returns a well-formed object: deployment requires registered authenticated adapters, protected service transport and current trust configuration. Empty/ambiguous/expired responses deny. Tests use explicitly synthetic adapters; external provider contracts and integration acceptance remain external gates, not silently completed tests.

Audit has no permission to modify clinical content. Service producers append only approved action codes within their tenant/producer scope. Adult patients query only their own bound grant projection; clinician disclosure permission does not grant receipt browsing. Logs omit payloads, credentials, cursor values and identifying metric labels. Audit attempts themselves are append-only; immutable correction is a new event. Data minimization retains only opaque references and bounded codes/times.

## Policy, recovery and interoperability

Retention, jurisdiction, patient-visible actor disclosure and approved issuers are typed configuration dependencies, not hard-coded universal legal rules. Use the MASTER17 DRAFT/APPROVED retention contract; missing/unapproved production policy blocks activation and deletion, while development uses labelled synthetic configuration. Hold evaluation is deny-deletion by default. Record actual policy approval separately; a filled approval_reference is not approval verification. This slice never deletes clinical source records.

Restore into quarantine; reconcile committed events, receipts, dedupe keys, stream digests and projection sequence epochs. Expire all restored cursor sessions. Conflicts or missing integrity checkpoint evidence keep reads unavailable. Rebuild projections only from verified committed events, replay outbox with idempotent keys, then run reconciliation acceptance. Backups, retention and independent checkpoint signing/key custody must be configured and accepted before production. An in-store digest alone cannot prove privileged-tamper resistance.

The API is internal EDHT JSON and does not export FHIR in this slice; no interoperability operation is necessary to read an already ingested record. External AuditEvent mapping remains a separate full-owner capability, with versioned semantic-loss tests before introduction. Unknown fields and unknown versions fail closed; incompatible changes use a new schema/API version. There is no legacy import in this profile. Future migration first dry-runs counts/key/digest reconciliation and preserves old receipt identity; rollback never rewrites acknowledged history.

## Events, observability, tests and acceptance

Audit outbox events carry opaque event/receipt IDs, tenant-internal routing, version and committed sequence; no clinical payload. They represent committed audit metadata, not proof of network delivery or clinician reading. No new clinical event is emitted by querying receipts. Grant/source changes are inputs from the existing MASTER17 lifecycle contract; Audit does not manufacture them.

Collect latency, denied/unavailable counts, outbox age, deduplication conflicts, projection lag and integrity checkpoint age with bounded aggregate labels. Proposed preproduction test load: 20 appends and 20 receipt queries per second for 10 minutes with <=100 items/page. Targets: p99 append <=500 ms, query <=1 second; zero acknowledged-event loss across injected restart; alert on persistent append failure or integrity mismatch within 60 seconds. These are acceptance targets, not measured service levels. Deployment must name on-call ownership and establish a measured load envelope.

Implementation acceptance fixtures: valid append/retry/conflict; cross-tenant and wrong-producer denial; patient self-query and clinician-only denial; snapshot paging and cursor expiry/policy change; concurrent revoke/read admission ordering; stale source/version denial; crash before/after commit and before response; outbox duplicate/out-of-order delivery; backup restore/reconciliation; log/cache redaction; nonrecursive receipt-query self-audit; patient display of attempt versus delivery; no source-fact write. Every failure denies or marks unavailable without leaking protected existence. Existing schema tests and pure contract oracles cover syntax and invariants only. End-to-end, fault-injection, load and security tests are requirements for implementation acceptance and remain unexecuted.

Clinical safety applicability is limited to accurate audit wording, patient identity and disclosure boundaries; no decision support occurs. Qualified review of these hazards, real issuer/role integration, approved legal/privacy configuration, cryptographic key custody and production security acceptance are explicitly external/deployment gates. The broader Audit owner's export/import/investigation gaps are engineering blockers outside this bounded MVP profile, not declared resolved.
